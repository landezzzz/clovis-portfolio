Keepay

A calm identity for a product that keeps your subscriptions in check.

Keepay is a subscription-intelligence app. It connects securely to a user’s bank to find, track and manage subscriptions and recurring payments, and warns people before a charge lands.

It is built for young professionals, students, freelancers, families and small teams who live on streaming platforms, online tools and SaaS, and want to stop being surprised by their own bank statement.

It was a turning point for me: the project that changed how I approach branding.

Industry:  Fintech • SaaS
Year:  2026

The brief

The founder arrived with clear constraints: a deep red and a teal green already chosen, Poppins as the typeface, and a mark that had to work on a website, inside an app, as a browser-extension icon, on social media and in print.

The brief asked for clean, modern and minimal, reflecting trust and ease of use. The brands he admired (Notion, Spotify, PayPal, Apple) share one thing: clarity. What he wanted to avoid: cluttered, hard-to-read or aggressive marks.

  • Trust without the usual “bank” look
  • Must hold up as a tiny extension icon
  • Existing colours: red and teal
  • About a week from brief to deadline

The idea behind the mark

The symbol stacks rounded shapes (subscriptions, bank cards, workspaces) and holds them inside a rhombus that leans forward into an arrow. The rhombus gives structure and a quiet sense of value without fintech clichés; the forward lean is about staying ahead of payments. The geometry stays rounded and calm, so it holds up in one colour and at icon size.

“Many subscriptions brought into one clear, controlled system. Always present, never overwhelming.”

The pitch deck

I shaped the founder’s investor story into a 12-slide deck: his own experience of losing money to invisible subscriptions, the wider problem, the research, the solution and the product.

In the product

The identity carried into the live product: landing page, subscription calendar, reminders and filters, in a dark interface where red and teal do the signalling.

Two years of labels, 3D product visuals and launches for a natural hair-care brand.

I have worked with the brand since 2024, from its first labels to a rebrand, 3D product visuals and new product launches.

One label system

Every label follows the same structure: the Dexsy’s portrait logo, the product name as the hero, ingredients, directions and benefits in a fixed order, a volume badge on a gold band, and a green footer with social handles and “Made in Cameroon”.

New products slot in without redesigning the range, which is exactly what happened when the herbal mask and growth spray launched in 2026.

The logo update

The original mark was a full-colour illustration wrapped in leaves: rich, but hard to print small or in one colour. The update keeps the same woman, afro and hoop earrings, redrawn as a single clean line drawing with a lighter wordmark, so it works on a label, a stamp, a bag or a phone screen.
